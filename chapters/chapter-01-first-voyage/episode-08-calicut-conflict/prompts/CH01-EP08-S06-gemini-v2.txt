MASTER_STYLE_02. 24mm lens, wide environmental naval shot from the stern rail of a Portuguese ship, glassy noon calm under a towering black squall wall, 30 August 1498. In the middle distance, three weathered Portuguese ocean-going carracks with deep wooden hulls, high sterns, each with two masts carrying single square sails and one lateen-rigged mizzen mast, no topsails, becalmed with limp canvas a league off Calicut. Between and beyond them, a wide arc of low wooden shore boats rows forward, every crewman in red folded-quilted cotton, all red clothing confined to the boats. In the right foreground, seen from behind, two Portuguese gunners in plain grey wool doublets and linen shirts serve one short bronze muzzle-loading bombard lashed with rope to the wooden rail, no wheeled carriage, one gunner holding a smoking linstock, powder smoke drifting. European historical oil painting, glassy swell, scorched bronze, wet sailcloth, no gunports, no explosions, no red clothing on the Portuguese ship, no modern ships.
