### SCENE 1: CH01-EP08-S01 — Detained on the Road (29–30 May 1498)
* **Location:** Road from Calicut to the Pandarani anchorage, Malabar Coast.
* **Source Basis:** The *Roteiro* records that after the 28 May audience, local men detained Gama "a night and a day on the road"; he wrote to his brother aboard ship to send goods, and on their receipt was allowed to re-embark, two men remaining with the landed goods. The narrator thanks God for extrication "from the hands of people who had no more sense than beasts."
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH (detention and release mechanism); MEDIUM (exact calendar days).
* **Characters:** `VASCO_01`, local Calicut officials and escorts (non-recurring, no invented IDs), thirteen landing-party men (background).
* **Ships:** `SHIP_SG01` at the Pandarani anchorage (distant).
* **Environment:** Humid coastal road and river-bank, palanquin rest-stop, crowded onlookers; ship's boat waiting at the water.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] Overnight detention, letter to his brother, release on receipt of goods; [HISTORICAL] Audience protocol and palanquin escort; [RECONSTRUCTION] Roadside setting, faces of officials; [CINEMATIC] Close framing of Gama writing the letter by lamplight.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"Late May 1498. Returning from his audience with the Zamorin, Vasco da Gama was held a night and a day on the road to Pandarani. He wrote to his brother aboard the flagship to send goods ashore — and only when they arrived was he freed to rejoin his ships."*

### SCENE 2: CH01-EP08-S02 — The Factory Ashore (24 June – 13 August 1498)
* **Location:** Portuguese merchandise house, first Pandarani then Calicut.
* **Source Basis:** The *Roteiro* ("Merchandise removed to Calecut, June 24") records goods carried to Calecut at the king's expense, crews visiting town by turns, private sales of shirts and cloth at steep losses, and purchases of "cloves, cinnamon, and precious stones... for samples." Gama left "a factor with the merchandise, together with a clerk and some other men" — Diogo Dias's post.
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH.
* **Characters:** `DIOGO_01` (Diogo Dias, factor), clerk and assistants, Malayali merchants, townspeople along the road.
* **Ships:** None.
* **Environment:** Sunlit warehouse with coarse woven sacks, brass scales, folded cloth and copper; busy street with fish-sellers and children; crews bartering bracelets and shirts.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] Factory, turn-taking shore visits, sample purchases; [HISTORICAL] Calicut as staple port — pepper and ginger local, cinnamon via Ceylon, cloves via eastern networks; [RECONSTRUCTION] Scales, bargaining gestures; [CINEMATIC] Sunlight on pepper dust.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"For seven weeks the Portuguese kept a small trade post at Calicut. Their cloth and copper fetched little — a fine shirt worth 300 réis at home went for thirty. What they carried back aboard was modest: samples of cloves, cinnamon, and precious stones, bought to show the King of Portugal what this market held."*
* **Audio Direction:**
  > `[VOICEOVER: immersive, curious, documentary cadence]`
  > `[AMBIENCE: marketplace banter, copper scales clinking, rustling sacks]`
  > `[FOLEY: peppercorns rattling into bowls, quill on manifest]`

### SCENE 3: CH01-EP08-S03 — The Factor Detained (13 August 1498)
* **Location:** Merchandise house, Calicut; roadstead aboard the ships (news arrival).
* **Source Basis:** The *Roteiro* ("Diogo Dias Carries a Message, August 13") records Dias bearing amber, coral and gifts plus a request for spice samples; the king, "with a bad face," demanded 600 xerafins as the country's custom. Leaving the palace, men followed Dias to the merchandise house, confined watchers with him, and proclaimed no boats might approach the ships. A servant reached the fleet by night with fishermen for three fanões — "Monday, the 13th August, 1498." The account blames Mecca and Cambay merchants who told the king the Portuguese were thieves, "offered rich bribes to the king to capture and kill us," and notes locals spitting and saying "Portugal, Portugal."
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH.
* **Characters:** `DIOGO_01`, Zamorin's factor and watchers, Mecca/Cambay merchants (background), the fisherman and messenger.
* **Ships:** Fleet at anchor (distant, S03 coda).
* **Environment:** Palace doorway glimpsed, then the shuttered merchandise house; night coast, fishing boats, dark water to the ships.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] 600-xerafin demand, confinement, proclamation, night messenger; [HISTORICAL] Meccan/Cambay merchant lobbying and bribe offers, as the account itself reports; [RECONSTRUCTION] Interior layout, faces; [CINEMATIC] The fisherman's night row to the fleet.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"13 August 1498. Diogo Dias carried gifts to the king and asked leave to sail. The king demanded six hundred xerafins — the custom of the country. Returning to the warehouse, Dias found guards posted at his door, and a proclamation forbidding any boat to reach the ships. That night a servant slipped past the watch and bought passage to the fleet for three small coins."*
* **Audio Direction:**
  > `[VOICEOVER: urgent, alarming, sudden crisis]`
  > `[AMBIENCE: shouting in the streets, shutters barred, night water lapping]`
  > `[FOLEY: doors barred, coins counted, paddle strokes in the dark]`
  > `[MUSIC: single suspense string line over deep drum]`

### SCENE 5: CH01-EP08-S05 — The Exchange (23–28 August 1498)
* **Location:** Roadstead between Calicut and the fleet; captain's longboat.
* **Source Basis:** The *Roteiro* records beating off to leeward 23 August; the 26 August parley with threats of bombards and the captives' heads unless Dias and his men — "or at least a letter" — were produced; the king's reversal, blaming his factor (claiming he lately killed another factor for extortion); Dias writing the king's letter "with an iron pen upon a palm-leaf, as is the custom"; on Monday 27 August seven boats delivering Dias and the others into the captain's longboat; Gama handing over the S. Gabriel pillar for erection, freeing six distinguished prisoners and keeping six against the merchandise; on 28 August a Tunis Moor's refuge aboard and the striped-cloth parley, Gama answering he "cared nought for the merchandise, but wanted to take these men to Portugal."
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH.
* **Characters:** `VASCO_01`, `DIOGO_01`, clerks, Malabar envoys, Tunis Moor refugee.
* **Ships:** `SHIP_SG01`, `SHIP_B01` (fleet); seven shore boats; captain's longboat.
* **Environment:** Grey working roadstead; boats moving between shore and fleet; the padrão passed hand to hand.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] Parley dates, pillar handover, six freed and six kept, Tunis refugee, palm-leaf letter; [HISTORICAL] *Olla* writing custom; padrão practice (S. Rafael at Rio, S. Gabriel at Calicut); [RECONSTRUCTION] Boat positions, faces; [CINEMATIC] The palm leaf changing hands in the longboat.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"On the 27th of August, seven boats brought Diogo Dias out to the fleet — with a letter for the King of Portugal, written with an iron pen on a palm leaf: 'Vasco da Gama, a gentleman of your household, came to my country, whereat I was pleased. My country is rich in cinnamon, cloves, ginger, pepper, and precious stones. That which I ask of you in exchange is gold, silver, corals and scarlet cloth.' Gama freed six prisoners and kept six — living proof, to carry home."*
* **Audio Direction:**

  > `[AMBIENCE: evening surf, oars, sea breeze]`
  > `[FOLEY: dried palm leaf handled, boots on deck, stone pillar shifted]`

### SCENE 6: CH01-EP08-S06 — Seventy Boats (29–30 August 1498)
* **Location:** Off Calicut, about a league north of the city.
* **Source Basis:** The *Roteiro* records the 29 August council — the country, spices and stones found, cordial relations impossible, sail with the detainees as future intermediaries ("greatly rejoicing at our good fortune in having made so great a discovery"); on Thursday 30 August at noon, becalmed, "about seventy boats" in red folded-cloth cuirasses closed to bombard range, were fired on, followed an hour and a half, until "a thunderstorm... carried us out to sea" and they turned back.
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH.
* **Characters:** `VASCO_01`, `COELHO_01`, gun crews.
* **Ships:** `SHIP_SG01`, `SHIP_SR01`, `SHIP_B01`; ~70 shore boats (no period type-name assigned — the account says boats).
* **Environment:** Glassy calm breaking into dark squall cloud; red-clad crews at oars; black-powder smoke drifting; then driving rain and wind.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] Council resolve, seventy boats, bombard fire at range, thunderstorm escape; [HISTORICAL] Shipboard bombards (muzzle-loaders — no gunport broadsides, no carronades); [RECONSTRUCTION] Battle geometry; [CINEMATIC] Wide shot of the squall wall swallowing the fleet.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"On the 29th of August the captains resolved to sail — the land was found, and friendship was hopeless. Next day, becalmed a league north of Calicut, some seventy boats bore down on them, crews clad in red quilted cotton. The bombards fired when they came in range. They trailed the fleet for an hour and a half — until a thunderstorm seized the sails and carried the Portuguese out to sea."*

### SCENE 7: CH01-EP08-S07 — Anjediva (10 September – 5 October 1498)
* **Location:** Cannanore coast (Compia landing), Santa Maria islets, mainland coast watering-place, Anjediva Island.
* **Source Basis:** The *Roteiro* ("Anjediva, September 20 to October 5"; twelve days on the island, both inclusive, per Ravenstein) records: 10 September landing a one-eyed captive with an Arabic letter to the Zamorin at Compia, whose king Biaquolle warred with Calicut; 15 September erecting the third padrão (Santa Maria) amid friendly fish-sellers; 20 September watering and wooding on a hilly coast with six islets, a Christian youth showing a spring; 21 September cinnamon branches, fowls, milk and gourds; 22 September seizing an abandoned provisions vessel (coconuts, four jars of palm-sugar, arms, sand ballast), firing on seven grounded craft, later refusing 1,000 fanões and burning the prize; 23 September warning that Calecut vessels hunted them; island anchorage with Bérrio and the flagship careened (Raphael not), a ruined stone church, straw-roofed chapel with three black stones, hewn-stone tanks; mainland fish, pumpkins, cucumbers, green cinnamon-wood; departure Friday, October 5.
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH.
* **Characters:** `VASCO_01`, `PAULO_01`, `COELHO_01` (watering party), crews, coastal Christians, mainland sellers.
* **Ships:** `SHIP_SG01` and `SHIP_B01` heeled ashore; `SHIP_SR01` afloat; captured prize (burned).
* **Environment:** Populated coast and island — chapel, tanks, villages and markets across the water; careening beach with hulls exposed, scrapers, water casks.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] All dated beats above; [HISTORICAL] Careening practice, padrão sequence; [RECONSTRUCTION] Labour arrangement, faces; [CINEMATIC] Hulls mirrored in the lagoon at dawn.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"The fleet worked north up an inhabited coast — a letter delivered to a king at war with Calicut, a stone pillar raised among friendly fishermen, cinnamon cut fresh from the wood. At Anjediva they hove down the flagship and the Bérrio, scraped their hulls, filled their casks from stone tanks, and burned a captured prize rather than sell her back. Twelve days. On the 5th of October they sailed for home."*

### SCENE 8: CH01-EP08-S08 — The Man Who Spoke Venetian (late September 1498)
* **Location:** Anjediva Island beach and beached vessel; open sea (confession coda).
* **Source Basis:** The *Roteiro* records a man of about forty, good Venetian, linen-clad with touca and sword, embracing the captains, claiming a western Christian long in India, servant of the Sabayo of Goa (40,000 horse), outwardly a Moor but Christian at heart, begging a cheese and two loaves as token, contradicting himself; Paulo da Gama's inquiry returning "pirate" with ships waiting; seizure to the beached vessel, thrashing and three- or fourfold "questioning," gestures betraying reconnaissance of ships and arms; at sea, his confession — sent to study the fleet and entice it into his master's power for wars with neighbouring kings. Ravenstein's Appendix E adds: carried to Portugal and baptized Gaspar da Gama; King Manuel's "Jew, turned Christian, merchant and lapidary"; Sernigi's "Sclavonian Jew, born at Alexandria"; Barros/Goes's Posen-parentage account (contested); later service as interpreter, household cavalier, 170-ducat pension.
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH (arrival, interrogation, sea confession); MEDIUM (Goa-court details as told); LOW (Polish/Poznań parentage — Barros/Goes only, contradicted in part by Sernigi).
* **Characters:** `GASPAR_01` (~40, linen, touca, belted sword — per account, not kaftan/boots), `VASCO_01`, `PAULO_01` (whose inquiry unmasks him), guards.
* **Ships:** `SHIP_SG01` (beached during careening).
* **Environment:** Working careening beach at sunset; crowd of crews; small boats from the mainland.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] As above; [HISTORICAL] Sabayo of Goa, Indian-Ocean Jewish merchant networks (framed as context, not identity proof); [RECONSTRUCTION] Conversation staging; [CINEMATIC] The cheese and loaves handed over as token.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"While the flagship lay on her side, a boat put ashore a man of about forty who greeted the captains in fluent Venetian. Linen-clad, sword at his belt, he embraced them like countrymen: a Christian from the west, he said, servant of the great lord of Goa. His story shifted with every telling. Paulo da Gama asked after him among his companions — and heard back a single word: pirate. They seized him on the sand and questioned him, again and again, until his gestures gave him away: he had come to study the ships. Far out at sea he confessed it plainly — his master wanted the Portuguese lured into his power. In Portugal he would be baptized Gaspar. Who he had been before that day remains disputed: a Sclavonian Jew of Alexandria, say some; a Pole of Poznań, say others. The journal itself never decides."*
* **Audio Direction:**
  > `[VOICEOVER: intrigued, skeptical, closing on uncertainty]`
  > `[AMBIENCE: lagoon evening, crickets, water against hulls]`
  > `[FOLEY: boat nudging sand, linen shifting, sword hilt gripped]`
  > `[MUSIC: solo lute dissolving into low drone]`

## Audit Note (v1 → v2, September 2026)

v1 was checked line-by-line against the Ravenstein transcription (Gutenberg pg46440) and voided for:
1. S01 invented "2 June ransom" and oath — the account records a night-and-day roadside detention after 28 May, release on goods received.
2. S03 wrong date (19 Aug) and chained-arrest staging — detention was 13 Aug by posted watchers and proclamation.
3. S04 wrong headcount/date (16 on 23 Aug) — eighteen seized on 19 Aug.
4. S05 "brother Vasco" — Diogo Dias was no kin of Vasco (the captain's brother is Paulo); exchange ran 23–28 Aug with six freed and six kept.
5. S06 "paraws," carronades, gunport broadsides, destruction claims — the account says boats, shipboard bombards, a 90-minute pursuit ended by thunderstorm.
6. S07 "uninhabited," "20 September," "two weeks," mountain springs — a populated island, 24 Sept–5 Oct (12 days), stone tanks.
7. S08 Poznań baptism-at-Anjediva "chief advisor" arc — origin contested, baptism in Portugal, household cavalier per Goes.
8. Anachronistic scoring (sitar, taiko, acoustic guitar) and unregistered CATUAL_01, "full steel armor," talwars, kaftan — all removed or re-marked.
