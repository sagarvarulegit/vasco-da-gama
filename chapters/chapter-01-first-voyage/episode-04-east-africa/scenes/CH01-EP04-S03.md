## Source Facts

[SOURCE] Fleet enters a broad river with low mud banks on 25 January 1498. They stay for 32 days. They beach and careen the *São Rafael* on a sandbank to clean her foul hull and repair the damaged mast and caulking.

## Historical Context

[HISTORICAL] The warm tropical waters of the Mozambique channel caused rapid marine growth (goose barnacles, green algae, and shipworm Teredo navalis). Careening (heeling the ship on its side at low tide) was perilous and backbreaking work under equatorial heat, especially with half the crew incapacitated by scurvy.

## Reconstruction

[RECONSTRUCTION] The massive hull of *São Rafael* tilted drastically on a muddy river sandbank; healthy crew and officers hauling tackle to support the masts while caulkers drive oakum and pitch into leaking hull seams amid billowing smoke.
