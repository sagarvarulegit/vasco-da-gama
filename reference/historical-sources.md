# Historical Sources

## Primary source

**Anonymous *Roteiro da primeira viagem de Vasco da Gama à Índia* (1497–1499).** The surviving manuscript is an anonymous, undated early-sixteenth-century copy of a voyage account believed to have been written aboard ship; the lost original has traditionally been attributed to Álvaro Velho, but attribution remains disputed. It is the principal source for route, dates, encounters, observations, and the expedition’s sequence.

**E. G. Ravenstein (ed./trans.), *A Journal of the First Voyage of Vasco da Gama, 1497–1499*, Hakluyt Society, 1898.** English translation and editorial apparatus used as the working text. Ravenstein’s introduction, appendices, muster roll, ship/equipment notes, maps, and alternate Portuguese accounts must be read as nineteenth-century editorial material, not as identical eyewitness evidence.

## Supporting context

- Luís de Sá Fardilha and Maria de Lurdes Correia Fernandes, eds., *Roteiro da Primeira Viagem…* (U.Porto Press, 2016), a modern Portuguese/English edition and study.
- Contemporary or near-contemporary Portuguese accounts reproduced in Ravenstein’s appendices (Girolamo Sernigi’s letters and later Portuguese narratives) are corroborative context, not substitutes for the *Roteiro*.
- Research on Indian Ocean maritime networks, Malabar trade, Swahili ports, and late-fifteenth-century Portuguese navigation should be added per scene; avoid treating modern summaries as primary evidence.

## Source discipline

Every scene separates `[SOURCE]`, `[HISTORICAL]`, `[RECONSTRUCTION]`, and `[CINEMATIC]`. The account is not called Vasco’s personal diary. Names, dates, ship identities, authorship, crew totals, and details of people/architecture are flagged where the evidence is incomplete or editorially mediated.
