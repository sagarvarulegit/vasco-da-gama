# CH01-EP01 — Lisbon: The Departure

**Format:** Immersive historical-painting video  
**Target duration:** 6 minutes 30 seconds  
**Active visual style:** MASTER_STYLE_02  
**Image source:** CH01-LOCK-v1 approved frame set  
**Narrative position:** The viewer travels within the working world of the expedition; the camera observes rather than invents private thoughts.

**Image-to-video prompts:** [Episode 01 prompt pack](../prompts/episode-01-image-to-video.md)

## Episode objective

Place the viewer on the Tagus waterfront and aboard the fleet on 8 July 1497. The episode should make departure feel physical: timber, rope, cargo, tide, wind, labor, and uncertainty. Portugal is leaving a settled maritime world and entering a difficult ocean route—not discovering an empty planet.

## Voice and narration rules

Measured documentary voice, intimate but not theatrical. No invented quotations. Narration is based on the surviving first-voyage account and broader historical context; reconstructions are labeled in production notes, not presented as eyewitness dialogue.

**Narration:**

**Narration:**

> On 8 July 1497, the armada left Belém. The departure itself was made of ordinary actions: a line released, a sail taking wind, a hull answering the tide. The extraordinary part was the distance those ordinary actions set in motion.

**Image:** [CH01-S06-v1.png](../images/episode-01/CH01-S06-v1.png)  
**Camera:** Sternward tracking shot; let the wake lead the eye back toward Lisbon, then widen toward open water.

**Narration:**

> Lisbon did not vanish into an unknown fantasy land. It receded behind a fleet entering an ocean whose winds, currents, and coasts had to be read correctly. The route south would carry the expedition away from the familiar shore and toward the great turn around Africa.

**Narration:**

> By night, the voyage became a discipline of attention. A lantern marked the wet deck. Sailors listened to timber and rope. Beyond the darkness, the other ships were only small lights on the water—close enough to reassure, far enough to disappear.

> The fleet took its southward course. Ahead lay not a blank horizon, but a chain of navigational problems: winds to find, water to preserve, storms to endure, and a continent whose southern edge would decide whether the road to India could be opened. The journey had begun.
