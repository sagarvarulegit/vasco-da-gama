* **Ships:** `SHIP_SG01`, `SHIP_SR01`, `SHIP_B01`.

### SCENE 2: CH01-EP09-S02 — The 84-Day Agony: Scurvy Decimates the Fleet
* **Location:** Lower deck of `SHIP_SG01`.
* **Source Basis:** *Roteiro* explicitly records 30 men dying of scurvy and only 7 or 8 sound men per vessel capable of working sails.
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH.
* **Characters:** `VASCO_01`, `PAULO_01`, emaciated and dying crewmen.
* **Ships:** `SHIP_SG01`.
* **Environment:** Cramped, dark ship hold; swaying lantern shadows; emaciated sailors lying on soiled burlap pallets.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] 30 deaths and extreme crew debility recorded in *Roteiro*; [HISTORICAL] Pathology of scurvy after 80 days without fresh provisions; [RECONSTRUCTION] Paulo consoling sailors; [CINEMATIC] Chiaroscuro candlelight illuminating hollow cheeks.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"Week after week, the dead ocean refused to let them go. Scurvy returned with monstrous brutality. Gums grew so thick and rotten over sailors' teeth they could not chew dry biscuit. Limbs blackened with gangrenous sores. Thirty men died, and on each ship, only seven or eight sailors had the strength to touch a rope."*
* **Audio Direction:**
  > `[VOICEOVER: heartbreaking, intimate, mournful documentary narration]`  
  > `[AMBIENCE: slow rhythmic groaning of oak ribs, water sloshing in bilge, muffled sea wind]`  
  > `[FOLEY: ragged labored breathing, wooden cup rattling in trembling hand, tearing cloth bandages]`  
  > `[MUSIC: profoundly tragic solo cello melody with low choir drone]`

### SCENE 6: CH01-EP09-S06 — The Citrus Miracle of Malindi (7 January 1499)
* **Location:** Malindi roadstead, Kenya.
* **Source Basis:** *Roteiro* records arriving at Malindi on 7 January; the friendly Sultan sent boats filled with oranges, lemons, eggs, and fowl.
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH.
* **Characters:** `VASCO_01`, Sultan's envoys, emaciated Portuguese sailors eating oranges.
* **Ships:** `SHIP_SG01` at anchor in Malindi bay.
* **Environment:** Sparkling tropical turquoise lagoon, bright morning sun, palm trees swaying.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] Sultan sending oranges and fresh provisions to cure crew; [HISTORICAL] Rapid reversal of scurvy symptoms via vitamin C; [RECONSTRUCTION] Crew reactions; [CINEMATIC] Close-up of tears streaming down a sailor’s face as sweet citrus juice cures his mouth.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"Five days later, the dying armada dropped anchor at friendly Malindi. The Sultan instantly dispatched boatloads of sweet oranges, lemons, and fresh meat. As starving men bit into the fresh citrus, their swollen gums began to heal within hours. The miracle of Malindi had saved the expedition from extinction."*
* **Audio Direction:**
  > `[VOICEOVER: warm, emotional, miraculous relief]`  
  > `[AMBIENCE: gentle lagoon lapping, tropical bird songs, laughing African oarsmen]`

### SCENE 8: CH01-EP09-S08 — The Midnight Pyre: Burning the São Rafael (20 January 1499)
* **Location:** Shoals of Mtangata (*Baixas de São Rafael*), near Tanga, Tanzania.
* **Source Basis:** *Roteiro* explicitly records burning *São Rafael* because there were not enough healthy crew members left to navigate three ships.
* **Historical Confidence:** HIGH.
* **Characters:** `PAULO_01` (weeping silently), `VASCO_01` (arm around his brother's shoulder), crew in boats.
* **Ships:** `SHIP_SR01` blazing with roaring fire; `SHIP_SG01` and `SHIP_B01` watching in silhouette.
* **Environment:** Midnight black ocean, colossal orange and crimson flames leaping 50 feet into the night sky, burning embers drifting across dark water.
* **Classification:** [SOURCE] Burning of *São Rafael* due to crew shortage; [HISTORICAL] Emotional heartbreak of Paulo da Gama losing his ship; [RECONSTRUCTION] Staging on boat; [CINEMATIC] Master tragic silhouette shot.
* **Narration & Voiceover:**
  > *"20 January 1499. The final tragedy of the Indian Ocean. With over half their comrades dead in the sea, there were simply not enough men left to sail three ships. At the shoals of Mtangata, Vasco gave the heartbreaking order to strip the São Rafael of her guns and set her ablaze. As flames consumed the great hull in the midnight blackness, Paulo da Gama wept for his ship—unaware that his own final voyage had already begun."*
