## Source Facts

[SOURCE] 11–12 April 1498: At night, Swahili swimmers from Mombasa armed with axes attempt to cut the anchor cables of the *Berrio* and *São Rafael*. The watchmen spot them in the water, shout alarms, and scare them off in boats. Vasco da Gama subjects two captured Moors from Mozambique to the drop torture (*pinga de azeite e pez a ferver* / boiling fat and pitch) aboard the flagship; under torture, they confess that the King of Mombasa intended to lure the vessels inside, capture the crews, and avenge the events of Mozambique.

## Historical Context

[HISTORICAL] Cable-cutting was a lethal maritime tactic; cutting a vessel loose at night in a coral roadstead would cause it to drift onto the reef and break up by morning. The use of judicial torture with boiling oil/grease was standard 15th-century Iberian naval practice when uncovering mutiny or treason.
