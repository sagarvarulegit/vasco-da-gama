## 1. Scope & Historical Narrative

Episode 9 chronicles the catastrophic return crossing of the Arabian Sea against adverse winter winds, the devastating scurvy epidemic, and the sacrifice of *São Rafael*:

1. **Departure from India (5 October 1498):** The three ships leave Anjediva, sailing into the open ocean without an experienced local pilot.
2. **The 84-Day Calamity (Oct–Dec 1498):** Trapped in relentless doldrums and adverse headwinds, the passage drags on for nearly three months (84 agonizing days vs. the 23-day outward voyage).
3. **The Scurvy Slaughter (Nov–Dec 1498):** Scurvy decimates the crews; 30 men die, gums swell over teeth, and limbs rot. Only 7 to 8 sound men remain per ship to work the sails.
4. **The Cry to Turn Back (Dec 1498):** Desperate sailors beg Vasco da Gama to turn back to India to die on dry land; Vasco and Paulo da Gama hold the fleet together through sheer iron will and prayer.
5. **Landfall at Mogadishu (2 January 1499):** Sighting the towering white stone palaces and mosques of Mogadishu (*Magadoxo*); the Portuguese bombard the hostile city with naval cannons from a distance.
6. **The Haven of Malindi (7 January 1499):** The dying crews arrive at Malindi, where the Sultan delivers baskets of sweet oranges, lemons, eggs, and fresh meat, miraculously arresting the scurvy.
7. **The Padrão of Malindi (11 January 1499):** Vasco erects a white limestone stone pillar (*Padrão de São Rafael*) on the Malindi promontory as an enduring monument to Portuguese-African alliance.
8. **Burning the São Rafael (13–20 January 1499):** Anchored off the shoals of Mtangata, with crew numbers too depleted to sail three ships, Vasco da Gama orders the beloved *São Rafael* stripped of guns and set on fire in a solemn midnight sacrifice.
