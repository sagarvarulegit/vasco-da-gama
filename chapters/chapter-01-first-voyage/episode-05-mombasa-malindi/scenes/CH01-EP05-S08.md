## Source Facts

[SOURCE] 22–24 April 1498: The King of Malindi delivers to Vasco da Gama a skilled pilot (*Christian of Gujarat/Cambay*, traditionally referred to as *Malemo Canaqua*). The pilot inspects the Portuguese navigational instruments (astrolabes) and displays his own nautical charts and celestial measurement devices (*kamal*). The Portuguese erect a stone pillar (*padrão*) dedicated to São Rafael on a headland near the town. On Tuesday, 24 April 1498, the fleet weighs anchor from Malindi and stands out into the open Indian Ocean, setting a direct course east-north-east toward India.

## Historical Context

[HISTORICAL] The monsoon navigation across the Arabian Sea was a sophisticated science practiced for centuries by Indian, Arab, and Persian mariners using the *kamal* (a rectangular wooden card with a knotted cord used to measure the height of stars above the horizon). Securing this pilot was the decisive breakthrough of the entire expedition.
